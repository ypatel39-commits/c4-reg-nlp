SEC Final Rule, Release No. 33-11275: The Enhancement and Standardization of Climate-Related Disclosures for Investors (March 2024)

Section 1. Disclosure of Material Climate-Related Risks. A registrant must disclose any climate-related risks that have had or are reasonably likely to have a material impact on the registrant, including on its strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. The disclosure must describe the actual and potential material impacts of any identified climate-related risks on the registrant's strategy, business model, and outlook.

Section 2. Governance and Oversight of Climate-Related Risks. The registrant must describe the board of directors' oversight of climate-related risks and management's role in assessing and managing material climate-related risks. This includes identifying any board committee or subcommittee responsible for the oversight of climate-related risks and describing the processes by which the board or such committee is informed about climate-related risks.

Section 3. Risk Management Processes. A registrant must describe the processes it uses for identifying, assessing, and managing material climate-related risks, and whether and how any such processes are integrated into the registrant's overall risk management system or processes. The registrant should disclose how it determines the relative significance of climate-related risks compared to other risks.

Section 4. Greenhouse Gas Emissions Disclosure. Larger filers (LAFs and AFs) are required to disclose their Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions on a phased-in basis, when those emissions are material. The disclosed emissions must be subject to an attestation report at the limited assurance level, transitioning to reasonable assurance for LAFs in subsequent years. Registrants must describe the methodology, significant inputs, and significant assumptions used to calculate the disclosed emissions.

Section 5. Financial Statement Effects. Registrants must disclose in a note to their audited financial statements certain disaggregated climate-related financial statement effects, including capitalized costs, expenditures expensed, and losses arising from severe weather events and other natural conditions. Internal controls over financial reporting must be designed to ensure the completeness and accuracy of these disclosures.

Section 6. Internal Controls and Attestation. Registrants must maintain internal controls and procedures that provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of climate-related disclosures. Management must evaluate the effectiveness of disclosure controls and procedures relating to climate-related disclosures, and any material weakness identified must be promptly remediated and disclosed.
